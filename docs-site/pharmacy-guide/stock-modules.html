Stock Balances, Movements & Counts

These three pages form the backbone of stock accuracy in Pulse. Used together they answer: what should we have, what did we do with it, and what do we actually have?

Stock Balances​

Stock Balances shows the period ledger per product:

ColumnMeaning
OpeningStock at the start of the period
ReceivedGoods received during the period
DispensedSold/dispensed during the period
ClosingOpening + received − dispensed − adjustments
ValueClosing quantity × price (ZMK)
DOSDays of stock remaining at current usage

Per-row actions​

Each row offers three quick actions:

  • History — the movement ledger for that product, with reasons and references.
  • Transfer — move stock between outlets of the same pharmacy.
  • Adjust — correct the closing balance with a reason (damage, theft, found stock, count correction). Every adjustment is written to the movement ledger with the acting user's name.
Where is Stock Balances?

Stock Balances is a pharmacy-side page. Pulse network users instead see Stock Balance Visibility, the network-wide equivalent — the two account sides are kept separate.

Stock Movements​

The complete, append-only audit trail of stock changes:

  • Receipts — from Goods Received notes.
  • Dispenses — from sales and dispensing flows.
  • Adjustments — manual corrections with mandatory reasons.
  • Transfers — between outlets.
  • Sale returns — reversals, written back into stock automatically.

Filter by movement type, product, or date range. The reference column links each movement to its source document (sale ID, GRN number, count ID) so any discrepancy can be traced to its origin in seconds.

Stock Counts​

Stock Counts runs the cycle-count workflow:

  1. Start a count — choose full inventory or a filtered subset (by category, outlet, or product).
  2. Enter physical quantities — the System column shows the expected quantity live as you count.
  3. Submit the count — pressing Submit Count files a report for every record audited up to that point. Items you did not reach are left untouched.
  4. Variance review — differences between system and counted quantities become adjustment suggestions; approve them to post corrections to the movement ledger.
Count in waves

Because Submit Count reports everything audited so far, large inventories can be counted in waves — morning shelves, afternoon fridge — without a single marathon session.
